# Literature verification and contribution assessment

Verified on 16 October 2026. The comparison below uses original papers and official publication records. It is a scoped review of the closest works identified in the available research discussions, not a claim of an exhaustive systematic review. Published results described here are the authors' reported findings; we did not reproduce their external experiments.

## Revised contribution

The defensible contribution is an auditable empirical comparison on a declared six-model panel: contextual finite-action prediction, independent versus group-regularized fits, generator transfer to unfitted elements, and controls that expose the interpretation limits of the measured recovery. The negative findings are substantive: rank-aware embeddings are nearly exact, globally averaged displacement cancels analytically, and source-conditioned additive steering is not outperformed.

This evidence does not establish that the semantic action is privileged over an equally predictive stable-category account. It also does not establish emergence through depth, a unique native group algorithm, broad compositional reasoning, superiority to manifold methods, or complete cross-model mechanism equivalence. The finite-orbit, propagation, and cancellation statements are elementary protocol diagnostics, not claimed advances in general representation theory.

The literature makes the remaining scientific risk sharper: several earlier papers have deeper theory or causal mechanism tests. A narrower, carefully documented comparative study may still be useful, but rewriting and adding references cannot supply the missing discriminating evidence. See `RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md` for prospective analyses; none is represented as already executed.
