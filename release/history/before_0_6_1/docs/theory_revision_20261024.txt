Decision

The report's common-design ridge identities, whitened independent-orientation
risk identity, full-rank commutant identity, and sample-mean learning curve are
correct under their stated restrictions. They are included as analytical
checks with proofs and explicit scope. They do not certify a new mechanism,
constitute LLM calibration measurements, or establish priority over prior
representation theory. No p-values, power certifications, or new empirical
measurements were derived from the constructions.

The new appendix distinguishes n states, r centered orbit dimensions, d
ambient coordinates, and the paper's existing q-dimensional PCA coordinates.
The D4 example is the four-corner action, with centered rank 3, not an
eight-state regular representation. All action equations use row vectors and
P_g P_h = P_(h composed with g), matching the existing paper and code.

Specific improvements

1. Common-design ridge identity, Appendix label app:ridge-identity.
   For L = (Z^T Z + alpha I)^(-1) Z^T, K = I - ZL, and R_g = L P_g Z:
     Z R_g - P_g Z = -K P_g Z;
     R_g R_h - R_(h composed with g) = -L P_g K P_h Z.
   These hold at every rank for alpha > 0. K is not generally an orthogonal
   projector. Element-specific source subsets, weights, penalties or target
   centering invalidate direct application of this particular common-design
   identity. The formulas describe unnormalized matrices, not the existing
   normalized held-out scores or independence of inferential evidence.

2. Geometry-preserving orientation construction, label app:orientation.
   Whitened state rows V and isometric context frames A_c give X_c = V A_c^T.
   Every X_c has the same within-context Gram matrix and exact local orthogonal
   action, while independent Haar orientation at a new context yields the
   stated character-dependent risk. The scalar optimum is over unrestricted
   shared matrix predictors and need not form a group representation.
   The C7 constants are 5/6 over all elements and 35/36 without the identity;
   the four-corner D4 constants are 7/9 and 8/9. They cannot be numerically
   compared with the empirical E_eq because the distribution and denominator
   differ. Cross-context Gram matrices are not preserved. The result does not
   show that every geometry-based method fails or that real activations obey
   the Haar assumption. Conditioning is on a fitted family independent of the
   new orientation, including any independent randomness used by its fitter.

3. Full-rank commutant identity, label app:commutant.
   At d = r, the exact optimal mean risk is the expected squared distance of
   relative context orientations from the action's commutant, divided by r.
   The proof explicitly uses closure under inverses for self-adjointness of
   group averaging, followed by the pairwise variance identity. Arbitrarily
   large commuting orientation changes may preserve a common action. The
   unrestricted least-squares mean need not obey group laws when its risk is
   nonzero. The result does not extend unchanged to rank-deficient, noisy,
   nonorthogonal or unwhitened LLM coordinates.
   The accompanying (1 - 1/m)b and (1 + 1/m)b learning curves describe iid
   sample means of local matrices in this same special construction only.

4. Existing statements rechecked.
   The finite-orbit construction, left-kernel invariance criterion, rank-
   deficient ambient extension, state-conditioned telescoping rule, faithful
   versus free D4 distinction, and coordinate/metric caution are consistent
   with the row-vector convention. The invariant-metric formula is valid for
   exact finite-group actions; it does not assert Euclidean orthogonality of
   approximate fitted operators. No changes to original results were needed.

Reference and priority assessment

The two newly added references were checked against primary records:

- Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups (1977), Graduate Texts in
  Mathematics 42, Springer New York; DOI 10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7.
  https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7
  This supplies standard representation-theory background. It is not cited as
  a source claiming the exact LLM construction or a modern statistical test.

- Kornblith, Norouzi, Lee and Hinton, Similarity of Neural Network
  Representations Revisited, ICML 2019, PMLR 97:3519-3529.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kornblith19a.html
  The primary record states the degeneracy of invertible-linear-invariant
  comparison when dimension exceeds the number of data points. This supports
  a brief distinction between high-dimensional interpolation and meaningful
  representation comparison, not a causal or transport conclusion.

The existing Sun reference was also checked in its original version:
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18190v1
Yuan Sun, Transformation Laws in Neural Representations: Structure,
Realisability, and Construction (2026). The paper directly covers induced
actions, encoder-fibre/kernel conditions, discarded information, shared
regional compatibility and composition. Its scope overlaps the existing
realizability interpretation. Accordingly, no “first”, “novel general
theorem”, or “new representation theory” claim has been added.

This is not an exhaustive theorem-by-theorem priority search. The derivations
are retained as elementary application-specific analytical boundaries, not
as a claim that no equivalent results exist elsewhere. The report's other
references are not automatically imported: only directly useful, verified
citations have been added. Its suggested stochastic tests, noise bounds and
new codebook experiments remain prospective and are not claims of this paper.

Deterministic checks

tests/test_theory_identities_20261024.py uses NumPy only, without models,
network access, random sampling, data extraction, calibration or refitting
any experiment. Five checks passed locally in approximately 0.06 seconds:

1. Common-design ridge identities at full and deficient rank, over every pair
   of noncommuting D4 actions, with an explicit opposite-multiplication check.
2. The independent-orientation risk calculation through a finite signed-
   permutation ensemble with exactly the required second moments; within-
   context Gram preservation and exact local orthogonality are also checked.
3. Centered C7 and four-corner D4 characters and all four risk constants.
4. The commutant projection, risk identity and a commuting positive control
   for a fixed finite weighted distribution of orientations.
5. Expected training and fresh-context sample-mean risks, by exhaustive finite
   enumeration for m = 1, 2 and 3.

The module has a zero-argument entry point compatible with tests/run_cpu_checks.py
and can also be run directly. These tests guard algebraic factors and action
conventions; they do not replace proof or imply empirical model validation.
