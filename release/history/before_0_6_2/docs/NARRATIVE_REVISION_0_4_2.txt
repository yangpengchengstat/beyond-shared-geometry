BEYOND SHARED GEOMETRY — PAPER RESTRUCTURING, VERSION 0.4.2
25 October 2026

Scope and outcome

This publication-only revision implements three changes to the reviewed 0.4.1 paper. It uses completed evidence and adds no model execution, fitting, experiment, calibration, bootstrap, statistical test or independent confirmation. Numerical results, failed runs and scientific configuration are preserved. A descriptive table renderer is new; the scientific runtime remains 0.4.0.

The manuscript is now titled “Beyond Shared Geometry: What Do Recovered Group Actions Identify in Language Models?” The retained contribution is an empirical audit of the explanations excluded or left compatible by a finite-action recovery protocol on a fixed panel. Elementary analytical identities clarify those limits. This revision does not claim a new general representation theorem, universal identification procedure, or successful native-mechanism discovery.

1. Reorganize around the scientific question

paper/main.tex, Abstract and Section 1:
State the central question before the experiment names: which interpretation is justified by prediction, approximate algebra and disrupted-registration separation? Identify the nearest transformation, calendar and mechanistic literature in the introduction. State three contributions at the same level as the completed evidence.

Sections 2–3:
Keep the row-vector convention, training-only PCA, optimization configuration, metric definitions, fixed model/template panel and inference scope visible. Present finite-orbit realizability, prediction-error propagation and global-field cancellation as elementary identification boundaries. The extended proofs remain in Appendix A, unchanged.

Section 4:
Organize completed evidence by what it tests:
4.1 prediction and consistency;
4.2 invalid versus coherent registration;
4.3 state information and storage;
4.4 context transport and behavioral interpretation.
FULL, AFS, characterization and replay names identify provenance. They are not successive certification stages.

The two historical depth curves and full structural/generator tables move to Appendix C (paper/sections/results.tex). Unit-budget compact comparisons join the complete budget curve in Appendix E (paper/sections/reproduction.tex). Extended related work moves to Appendix G (paper/sections/related_work.tex); the closest work remains cited in the introduction. Existing figures, values, references and theoretical proofs are retained.

2. Put the core contrasts in the main paper

Main Table 1, label tab:identification-summary, is generated by scripts/build_identification_table.py from already checked-in results. Its 46 source values have paths, hashes and selectors in paper/generated/identification_summary.source.json. These include values used to verify all budget crossovers, not only values printed in the table.

The table has six rows: independent prediction, excluded action-element transfer, restricted invalid-registration references, category controls, context differences, and compact nonlinear storage tradeoffs. Each row gives the observation, its supported conclusion and a remaining alternative.

Essential interpretation safeguards are retained:
- Historical target-normalized D, replay coordinate loss and replay context-difference loss have different denominators. They are not ranked against one another.
- Replayed estimates are not independent confirmations.
- Repeated splits reuse templates; 120 cells are not 120 independent discoveries.
- The restricted invalid-reference score is not a calibrated semantic-null p-value.
- The coherent alternative is a conjugated action evaluated on its own registered targets. It does not solve an identical target task or distinguish nonisomorphic abstract representations.
- Decoded centroids additionally receive training labels and the exact action table. They win in only 9/24 C7 and 2/24 D4 cells despite the favorable C7 mean.
- The entire recorded 0.5/1/2/4/8 storage curve remains available. Storage is not statistical capacity, total RAM or runtime. Crossovers do not define a test-selected deployment policy.
- Descriptively close means do not establish equivalence.

3. Provide an actionable interpretation framework

Section 5 distinguishes four complementary questions, not an implication ladder:

Predictive recovery: which targets were excluded from fitting, and what information and storage did the comparator receive?

Action consistency: which laws and metrics are assessed, were they encouraged in fitting, and how much consistency follows from prediction error and operator norms?

Preferential semantic alignment: does the specified correspondence outperform relevant coherent alternatives under explicit target definitions and information conditions? Preference over selected alternatives still does not establish uniqueness.

Native causal use: for a claim about successful task computation, is clean competence established and is the intervention faithful? Distinguish sufficiency, necessity and directional score movement. Failed task behavior can also have native causal mechanisms; competence is not asserted as a universal prerequisite for every causal question.

The paper supports the first two questions within its recorded scope. It leaves preferential semantic alignment unresolved and does not establish native causal implementation. This is an interpretation protocol illustrated by one panel, not a universally validated decision rule.

Assessment after restructuring: independent contexts

Decision for the retained manuscript: no new experiment is required now. The paper explicitly claims a fixed-panel descriptive case. Existing held-out-template prediction, coherent alternatives, category controls and storage/context comparisons support that case. Preserve the evidence and submit the revised narrative for author review before spending further GPU time.

Remaining competitiveness risk: clearer organization does not create new scientific novelty, statistical independence or mechanistic evidence. The strongest reviewer concern remains the significance of a narrow C7/D4 case relative to prior transformation and causal-feature work. This revision improves the presentation of an empirical contribution; it does not justify an acceptance probability or a prediction of high-probability acceptance.

One optional future experiment, only if broader contextual generalization is retained as an additional central claim:

- Independently author and prospectively freeze a disjoint context bank before evaluation, avoiding paraphrase-only expansion of the existing grammar and avoiding reuse of inspected prompts.
- Preserve the declared state vocabulary, model/readout panel, metric denominators, trained PCA, predictor parameters, decoder, action definitions and storage budgets. No refitting or budget selection on the new test bank. Any necessary technical validation uses separately excluded examples.
- Compare the frozen canonical predictor, a predefined coherent-action predictor on its own registered targets, and the declared category control, preserving their information differences. For context differences, form the paired contexts under the frozen rule and retain the same training-energy normalization.
- Predeclare one primary endpoint and the context-family unit of uncertainty. Report every frozen context family and failure; do not turn selected-model/readout cells into independent population samples. A sampling model is needed before claiming population coverage.
- Interpret the result only as a context-generalization check. It cannot by itself reject category coding, deliver a calibrated semantic null, establish native causal computation or repair blocked historical confirmations. Existing fits were selected using inspected evidence; the new bank would be independent evaluation of a frozen selected procedure, not an independent discovery history.

This optional evaluation is not implemented or launched by this revision. No additional calibration sweep or full rerun is proposed.
