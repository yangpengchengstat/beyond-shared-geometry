# Scientific scope

The paper studies recoverable finite-group actions on controlled language-model activations and downstream effects of applying learned operators. Its evidence concerns the declared tasks, prompt templates, pinned model panel, depths, splits, dimensions, controls, and metrics.

## Held-out evidence and imposed constraints

Template splits prevent direct reuse of training template IDs in evaluation. PCA and operator estimation use training activations. All category states nevertheless appear in training; this is template transfer, not unseen-category learning.

Joint fitting trains every group operator and includes all ordered homomorphism pairs, defining relations, identity, and orthogonality penalties. Low relation/composition residuals partly assess imposed constraints. `E_comp_data` compares a product of operators with another fitted operator on held-out activations. `E_word` is stronger because generator products are compared to natural transformed activations, but canonical generators still come from the fully supervised fit.

Stable category encodings can realize exact permutation representations. The existence of a low-defect action therefore does not by itself establish algebraic processing beyond category coding. Read FULL with AFS-A/B/C/D, prototype/embedding controls, and source-conditioned additive-field results. A failed or inconclusive falsification control must stay visible; a positive control is not proof of a unique mechanism.

## Activation interventions

The row-vector edit `h' = h - zC + (zR)C` preserves the orthogonal PCA residual. Operators are composed externally before the source activation is patched. Behavioral effects establish consequences of this controlled edit; they do not establish that the unedited model internally executes the same sequence of algebraic operations.

Target-logit/probability changes are distinct from full-answer accuracy. The canonical runner scores the first context-conditioned continuation token and records token counts; sequence log-probability is missing for multi-token labels. Source spans are mean-pooled and the same replacement vector is written at every span token. If a source spans multiple tokens, compare a pooled-span sham patch before attributing every difference to the intended operator.

The source-conditioned additive control uses training-template differences indexed by transformation and source state. It is stronger than a globally averaged displacement, which cancels on a balanced orbit. In canonical D4 relation rows it is indexed by the target endpoint, so agreement for true/direct endpoints is built into that baseline. State-aware additive fields also deserve direct and composition comparisons; success against identity/random operators alone does not establish superiority to them.

## Cross-model and statistical interpretation

The fitted intertwiner minimizes a homogeneous commutation residual with unit Frobenius norm. Rank, invertibility, full-subspace coverage, and predictive transport of activations are not required. A low residual can reflect a small shared component. The canonical fit and score use the same group labels; the shuffled control reuses the correctly fitted map. These are in-sample compatibility diagnostics, with limited evidence for full representation equivalence or unseen-action transfer.

Bootstrap intervals follow the recorded hierarchy, not an assertion that every layer, split, model, or model pair is independently sampled. Reused templates and model-pair endpoints create crossed dependence. Keep estimates conditional on the declared panel and distinguish split/template variability, deterministic fitting, and random-control variation.
