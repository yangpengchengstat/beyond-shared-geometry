# Scientific audit

The preserved experiments support recoverable approximate C7/D4 actions on fixed lexical states across held-out contextual templates. They do not identify a unique semantic computation beyond stable category coding. Code and archive checks did not find an established group-ordering error, train/test leakage, or corruption that invalidates the primary recovery result. The main scientific risks concern interpretation, novelty, inference scope, and missing discriminating comparisons.

## Analytical interpretation

A full-centered-rank finite code admits exact linear permutation actions. A stable state component with an invariant context component can therefore explain shared operators, generator transfer, and order-sensitive D4 behavior without uniquely identifying semantic computation. Prediction residuals also propagate into composition defects. These elementary observations motivate discriminating evaluations; they are not presented as new general representation theory.

The revised appendix makes coordinate and metric dependence explicit, relates multi-context realizability to a standard projection residual, and shows that source-conditioned centroid differences telescope when source identity is updated. Linear operators themselves can be written as state-dependent additive residual updates. Consequently, a broad opposition between additive fields and multiplicative structure would be misleading.

Data and operator defects retain their historical definitions: mean squared relative row errors versus unsquared relative Frobenius errors, including different C7 relation aggregands. Their magnitudes cannot be ranked directly. Identity operators satisfy algebra perfectly while failing nontrivial transport. Neither low algebraic residual alone nor a larger ambient residual determines semantic content.

## Novelty and readiness

The original papers reviewed in `LITERATURE_AUDIT.md` establish paired transformation recovery, neural group-operation circuits, calendar interventions, action/composition theory, and limitations of mechanistic-equivalence claims. The revised contribution is the specific comparative pretrained-LLM study and the interpretation of its positive and negative controls. It is not a first discovery of neural algebra, noncommutativity, calendar steering, or composition-error propagation.

Stronger semantic specificity requires matched stable-state alternatives, consistent alternative actions, prediction/norm calibration, and broader holdouts. Stronger mechanism claims require causal designs beyond score-moving edits; cross-model generalization requires held-out transport and matched null refitting. Matched structural field and generator comparisons have since run on excluded development data and informed the separately frozen characterization study. The independent characterization return has now been audited to the supported compact-coordinate level; reviewed descriptive aggregates are in the manuscript. The [21 October re-audit](PROJECT_REAUDIT_2026_10_21_V2.md) supersedes pending-status statements and records remaining limits. Native mechanism and held-out cross-model studies remain separate unfinished work. The manuscript's narrower conclusion is supported; competitive significance for ICML remains a scientific judgment that a successful build cannot settle.
