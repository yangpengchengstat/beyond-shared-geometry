What can recovered transformations reveal about language-model representations beyond predictive accuracy? Given a prescribed finite-group action on input states, we fit maps that predict hidden representations of transformed states from their source representations in the same context. Each operator family is shared across contexts within a fixed model-layer coordinate space. Our architecture-independent theory distinguishes optimal unconstrained linear prediction from minimum risk over fixed-dimensional exact orthogonal actions under a common second-moment-whitened metric. An analytical example gives two observation systems with identical optimal regression risk and eigenvalue spectra of their orbit Gram matrices, but different exact-action risks. A spectral budget respecting real irreducible representation types gives lower and upper risk bounds differing by at most a factor of two. We study weekday cycles ($C_7$) and square-corner actions ($D_4$) in six frozen, pretrained decoder-only autoregressive Transformers from the Llama and Qwen families. Linear recovery, label-informed category controls and compact nonlinear predictors test competing predictive explanations; the latter are not established nonlinear group actions. Finite-bank spectra and constructed exact-action witnesses connect the theory to measured representations. Frozen witnesses have higher held-out loss than independent least squares in all 48 conditions, while exhaustive permutation-image comparisons do not uniformly favor the specified semantic image. On new authored contexts under matched validation selection, difference fitting lowers mean weekday loss but raises mean square-corner loss relative to coordinate fitting. These results distinguish predictive recovery, exact-action compatibility and semantic interpretation without identifying a uniquely supported semantic action or native computation.
